## 3. User Journey
Instead of thinking of tasks in isolation, consider the experience as a story.  

- What is the sequence of actions taken?
- How does one step lead to another?
- What does the journey look like from start to finish?

**Key Concept:**  
This step creates **the second dimension** in your story map—helping you see not just priority, but also **flow and context**.
<PROMPT_END>

## 5. Prioritize (Define What Matters Most) (output: keep the same format as before but move all critical tasks to row 3, secondary tasks to row 4, and other tasks to row 5 and so on)

Once the user journey is mapped, **identify the most critical tasks first.**

**Prioritization Strategies:**
- **MVP (Minimum Viable Product)** → What’s the absolute minimum needed for success?
- **Time-Sensitivity** → What needs to happen first, second, third?
- **User Impact** → What delivers the most value with the least effort?

**Key Questions:**
1. What must happen for the process to work?
2. What is “nice to have” but not essential?
3. What can be postponed or removed?

## 6. Identify Iterations (Release in Phases)

Rather than doing everything at once, **break your plan into small, manageable iterations.**

- Each iteration should provide **real value**.
- Think of each phase as an **experiment**: learn, adjust, and improve.

**Key Idea:**  
Each iteration delivers something useful while allowing for **continuous learning and adaptation**.

## 7. Look for Gaps & Alternatives

Now that you have a structured map:
- Are there **missing steps** that were overlooked?
- Are there **alternative approaches** that achieve the same outcome?
- Are there dependencies that need adjusting?

**Key Outcome:**  
This step ensures that the roadmap is **robust and flexible**.
